Hyperbolicity of semi-dispersing billiards

UNSOLVED
Fields
Classical MechanicsBilliard Dynamics
Topics
semi-dispersive billiardhyperbolicity

Problem

Definitions

In a planar billiard table Ω⊂R2\Omega\subset\mathbb R^2Ω⊂R2, a unit-speed particle moves along straight lines and reflects by v↦v−2⟨v,n⟩nv\mapsto v-2\langle v,n\rangle nv↦v−2⟨v,n⟩n. The collision map TTT acts on Φ=∂Ω×(−π/2,π/2)\Phi=\partial\Omega\times(-\pi/2,\pi/2)Φ=∂Ω×(−π/2,π/2), with boundary arclength sss and angle φ\varphiφ to the inward normal. Its invariant probability measure is dμ=Z−1cos⁡φ ds dφ.d\mu=Z^{-1}\cos\varphi\,ds\,d\varphi.dμ=Z−1cosφdsdφ. Vertex hits and grazing collisions are excluded from the regular phase space.

A semi-dispersing table has smooth boundary pieces that are dispersing or flat. Hyperbolicity means nonzero Lyapunov exponents transverse to the flow almost everywhere.

Assumptions

The planar table is connected, has finite area, and contains a nonflat dispersing arc. A test class replaces some, but not all, sides of a polygon by dispersing circular arcs.

Claim

Is every such nondegenerate semi-dispersing billiard hyperbolic μ\muμ-almost everywhere?

Source

Gutkin's 2001 problem manuscript; Problem 4 of the updated survey.
