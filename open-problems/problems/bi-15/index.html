Classification of polygons with finite blocking

UNSOLVED

Problem

Definitions

In a planar billiard table Ω⊂R2\Omega\subset\mathbb R^2Ω⊂R2, a unit-speed particle moves along straight lines and reflects by v↦v−2⟨v,n⟩nv\mapsto v-2\langle v,n\rangle nv↦v−2⟨v,n⟩n. The collision map TTT acts on Φ=∂Ω×(−π/2,π/2)\Phi=\partial\Omega\times(-\pi/2,\pi/2)Φ=∂Ω×(−π/2,π/2), with boundary arclength sss and angle φ\varphiφ to the inward normal. Its invariant probability measure is dμ=Z−1cos⁡φ ds dφ.d\mu=Z^{-1}\cos\varphi\,ds\,d\varphi.dμ=Z−1cosφdsdφ. Vertex hits and grazing collisions are excluded from the regular phase space.

A polygon has finite blocking if, for every pair of interior points x,yx,yx,y, a finite set B(x,y)⊂P∖{x,y}B(x,y)\subset P\setminus\{x,y\}B(x,y)⊂P∖{x,y} meets every nonsingular billiard trajectory from xxx to yyy. A rational polygon unfolds to a compact translation surface S(P)S(P)S(P).

Assumptions

Treat rational and irrational polygons separately.

Claim

Classify polygons with finite blocking. In the rational class, characterize the unfolded surfaces as branched torus covers, and determine the corresponding polygonal or almost-integrable structure. Extend the classification to irrational tables.

Source

Monteil's 2004 preprint on finite blocking; Gutkin's updated survey, Problem 15.

Progress

  • Lelièvre, Monteil, and Weiss prove that a compact translation surface has finite blocking exactly when it is a branched torus cover. This settles the rational unfolding class; the general irrational classification remains open.
