Approximate hardness of random-circuit sampling

UNSOLVED
Fields
Quantum ComputingComputational Complexity
Topics
complexity

Problem

Definitions

Use uniform polynomial-size circuits and input bit length as the complexity parameter. BQP has no witness; QMA has a polynomial-qubit witness; QCMA has a polynomial-bit witness; QMA(2) has two unentangled quantum witnesses. Standard bounded-error verification has completeness at least 2/32/32/3 and soundness at most 1/31/31/3.

Assumptions

Square-grid nnn-qubit circuits with n3n^3n3 layers of Haar two-qubit gates; success on fraction at least 1−1/n1-1/n1−1/n, sampling variation error at most 1/n1/n1/n and fixed finite input precision.

Claim

Consider nnn qubits on a square grid, with n3n^3n3 layers cycling through the four horizontal/vertical even/odd matchings and independent Haar-random two-qubit gates. Prove that a polynomial-time classical sampler within total-variation distance 1/n1/n1/n of the ideal output on at least a 1−1/n1-1/n1−1/n fraction of these circuits would collapse the polynomial hierarchy, using only standard worst-case complexity assumptions.
